Our history

Two kinds of teacher, one classroom

Rashtriya Vidhyapith has been teaching children in Ratlam since 1941. Whatever has changed about the school over the years, it still measures itself by one standard: does the student understand, or have they only memorised?

We teach the full modern curriculum required of any school today, and alongside it, we keep alive the older methods this land taught with for centuries: recitation, dialogue, apprenticeship, and patience. Neither is treated as decoration for the other.

What we study

A modern curriculum, taught by hand

Classes 1 through 8, following the national curriculum framework, with smaller-than-usual sections so that a teacher can still know every student by name.

Social Sciences

Computing & Digital Literacy

How we teach

Three habits from the old Gurukul, still in use

None of these are museum pieces. Each one shows up somewhere in our timetable this week, adapted to a classroom of thirty rather than a courtyard of five.

1

Guru-Shishya Parampara — learning through relationship

Understanding was built through a long, direct relationship between one teacher and a few students, not a lecture to a hall.

THEN

A single teacher took full responsibility for a small group of students across many years.

NOW

Every student here is assigned a mentor-teacher who follows their progress across subjects, not just one.

2

Learning outdoors, under a tree

A courtyard or a tree's shade was the original classroom, chosen for quiet rather than walls.

THEN

Classes were held outdoors whenever weather allowed, seated in a loose circle around the teacher.

3

Shastrartha — learning by argument

Understanding was tested in open, reasoned debate, where a student had to defend an idea in front of others.

THEN

Scholars debated a text's meaning point by point, in front of an audience that judged the reasoning.

NOW

Every class holds a weekly debate period on a question from that week's lesson, no notes allowed.
